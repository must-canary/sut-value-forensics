## 0. Rules of this matrix

### 0.1 Allowed statuses (no others may be used)

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **SUPPORTED** | Evidence meets the stated test, alternatives considered, mechanism plausible |
| **REJECTED** | Evidence contradicts the hypothesis, or it cannot account for the observed magnitude |
| **INCONCLUSIVE** | Data exists but does not discriminate |
| **DATA UNAVAILABLE** | The required evidence could not be obtained |

### 0.2 Discipline

- A hypothesis may be **SUPPORTED** as a *contributing mechanism* without being *the cause*.
  Supported hypotheses are **not** mutually exclusive.
- **REJECTED** normally means "rejected as a *sufficient* explanation". The scope of any
  rejection is stated explicitly.
- **No overall root-cause verdict is produced by this matrix, and none may be generated by
  an AI model.** The matrix reports per-hypothesis evidence states. A human investigator
  draws conclusions.
- Correlation in timing is **never** promoted to causation, however strong.

## H1 — Broad crypto market conditions

**Statement.** The May 2026 SUT decline was caused by a broad crypto-market downturn
affecting all assets.

**Mechanism.** Market-wide risk-off → correlated drawdown across assets → SUT falls with
the market.

**Why plausible.** Small-cap tokens routinely show high beta to BTC/ETH. This must be
excluded before any SUT-specific claim is made.

**Required data.** SUT daily/intraday series; BTC, ETH over the identical window.

**Source.** SUT: GeckoTerminal pool `0x092295c9…e165` (`CONTRACT_VERIFIED`) + CoinGecko +
Coinranking. BTC/ETH: CoinGecko `market_chart/range`. Retrieved 2026-09-30.

**Calculation.** Index all assets to 100 at window start; compute relative and residual
returns over May 16 → May 18.

**Graph.** G1 — SUT vs BTC vs ETH, normalised to 100.

### Supporting evidence
- ETH drifted down ~7% across the full window (index 100 → 93.3), a mild risk-off tone.

### Contradictory evidence — decisive
| Asset | May 16 | May 18 | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| BTC | 78,161.07 | 77,016.44 | **−1.5%** |
| ETH | 2,179.90 | 2,134.55 | **−2.1%** |
| **SUT** | **0.6737** | **0.1308** | **−80.6%** |

SUT's move is **~40–55× the control assets'** over identical hours. BTC ranged only
99.3–103.8 (index) across the whole window; there was **no market-wide crash** on
2026-05-17/18.

**Limitations.** Daily closes for BTC/ETH; an intraday market dislocation on May 17 is not
fully excluded, though a ~1–2% daily range bounds it tightly. Only two controls used — a
small-cap altcoin index would strengthen the test.

### Status: **REJECTED** *(as a sufficient explanation)*
H1 is retained only as a minor background variable. It cannot account for the magnitude.

**Missing evidence.** Small-cap/altcoin index; intraday BTC/ETH for May 17.

## H2 — Liquidity / market-depth shock

**Statement.** SUT's tradable liquidity was so thin relative to its displayed market
capitalisation that an ordinary-sized sell order produced an extraordinary price move; the
decline was caused or amplified by depth failure rather than by a proportionate change in
valuation.

**Mechanism.** Displayed market cap = supply × price is an arithmetic product, not
realisable value. With a ~$91.7K DEX pool and a ±2% CEX book of ~$4.4K/−$12.0K, a sale of a
few tens of thousands of dollars can move price by tens of percent. Depth failure converts a
modest flow into a crash.

**Why plausible.** Directly measured structural fact: ~$75M displayed cap against ~$0.1M of
observable liquidity — a ratio of roughly 800:1.

**Required data.** DEX pool reserves over time; LP Mint/Burn events; CEX order-book depth
and spread during May 16–20; realised price impact per unit volume.

**Source.** GeckoTerminal (pool OHLCV + reserves, `CONTRACT_VERIFIED`); Uniswap V3 pool
contract logs; **CEX depth — no retroactive source identified.**

**Calculation.** (a) Price impact per $1K traded, daily. (b) Liquidity-to-market-cap ratio.
(c) Pool reserve time series across the event. (d) Simulated slippage at $10K/$50K/$100K.

**Graph.** G5 — price impact / liquidity before, during, after May 18.

### Supporting evidence
- **Market cap ≈ $75M vs main pool ≈ $91.7K** — displayed value exceeds observable DEX
  liquidity by ~800×.
- **Gate ±2% depth: +$4.4K / −$12.0K.** A $12K sell exhausts the 2% band on the venue
  carrying ~58% of volume.
- **Realised impact is consistent with depth failure.** On May 18 the *entire* main-pool
  volume was **$712,616** while price fell **−72.5%** on the close and traded as low as
  $0.1145. A sub-$1M flow moved a nominally $75M asset by ~80%.
- **⭐ DECISIVE — measured by procedure C1 (2026-09-30), from decoded Uniswap V3 `Swap` events.**
  On May 17 actual swap volume was **$784,333**, split **$378,676 buy / $405,657 sell** — a
  **net sell imbalance of just ~$26,981** — while price fell **62.7% intraday**. On May 18 the
  net imbalance was **$3,160** against a **−72.5%** close.

  > **A net directional imbalance of roughly $27,000 accompanied a 62.7% single-day collapse.**
  > Gross two-way volume was substantial and almost perfectly balanced. **The price did not
  > fall because of a large net sell imbalance — it fell because marginal depth to absorb order
  > flow was exhausted.** This is the H2 mechanism observed directly, not modelled, and it is
  > the single strongest piece of evidence in the case.

- **Corrected sell figures (C1 supersedes raw-transfer proxy):** SUT sold via swaps was
  **1,045,185** (May 17) and **1,041,222** (May 18) — **2,086,407 over two days**, not the
  2,461,147 previously stated, which had included LP deposits.
- Swap count rose from **246 (May 16) to 4,341 (May 17) and 4,392 (May 18)** — an ~18×
  increase in trading events.
- **Pre-existing fragility.** April 27 (before any May event): open 0.6199, intraday low
  0.4197 (−32%), recovering same day. May 17's −62.7% intraday excursion is the *same
  pattern, amplified* — evidence of a chronically fragile book rather than a novel shock.
- **Dead-pool structure.** ~11 dormant pools hold fragmented, unusable liquidity, further
  thinning effective depth.
- **Volume/market-cap ratio ~0.1%** persistently.
- **NEW — peer-relative confirmation** (`PIP_Week_2_thesis.pdf` pp.3–4, CMC data 2026-09-29/30):
  SUT's volume/market-cap is **0.11% against a #90–#110 median of 12.4% (~110× worse)**, and
  **even the least-traded coin in that band turns over ~50× more per day** ($4.1M vs ~$0.08M).
  SUT's illiquidity is not merely "small-cap normal" — it is **structurally abnormal for its
  displayed capitalisation**. `SECONDARY` confidence; peer figures single-source.

### Contradictory evidence
- Trading activity on May 17–18 was ~18× the May 16 level, so liquidity *demand* spiked; thin
  depth explains amplification but **does not explain why the selling started**.
- **⚠ The "LPs pulled liquidity" variant is now REJECTED.** C1 measured LP `Mint`/`Burn`
  directly: liquidity churn rose ~65× on May 17 (26 mints/30 burns → **643/679**) but **net
  liquidity was positive every day of the crash** — May 17 **+$15,911**, May 18 **+$3,667**,
  May 19 **+$3,053**. The churn is the signature of **concentrated-liquidity re-ranging** as
  LPs followed a falling price, **not** withdrawal. **Liquidity providers did not flee.**

**Limitations.**
- **CEX depth/spread for May 2026 is DATA UNAVAILABLE** and probably permanently so.
- **May 2026 pool TVL was never measured and remains unmeasured.** The ~$91.7K liquidity and
  ±2% book figures are **2026-09-29/30 snapshots**, not May observations. **The earlier claim
  that selling "exceeded depth by more than an order of magnitude" is withdrawn** — it was
  anchored to a September TVL figure. The finding above (net ~$27K imbalance → −62.7%) is the
  defensible statement and does **not** depend on any TVL estimate.
- **Single-day volume splits for May 17/18 are unreliable** (conflict C15, day-boundary drift
  in interpolated timestamps). **Use the combined two-day figure** ($1,147,309 swap volume),
  which agrees with GeckoTerminal within 3.3%.

### Status: **SUPPORTED** *(as an amplification mechanism — not as the trigger)*
**CEX limb: DATA UNAVAILABLE.**

> H2 explains **how** a modest flow became an 80% move. It does **not** explain **what**
> initiated the selling. H2 and H3 are complementary, not competing.

**Missing evidence.** Q5 (LP Mint/Burn events across May); pool reserve time series; May-dated
CEX depth/spread (likely unobtainable); per-venue volume split (Q6).

## H3 — Concentrated holder selling / distribution

**Statement.** Selling or distribution by large, concentrated holders caused or amplified
the decline.

**Mechanism.** Top-10 holders control ~71% of supply. A large holder moving tokens to a
venue and selling into a ~$91.7K pool would collapse the price.

**Why plausible.** Concentration is extreme, and a large, sustained, near-daily SUT pipeline
ran through the exact window from a **top-10 holder**.

**Required data.** Holder balance changes across May; transfer graph for the cluster; **DEX
Swap events matching cluster addresses**; CEX deposit-address identification.

**Source.** Polygon RPC `eth_getLogs` (Transfer + Swap topics); Blockscout; GeckoTerminal.

**Calculation.** (a) Net flow per cluster address per day. (b) Cross-match cluster addresses
against Swap events in pool `0x092295c9…e165`. (c) Lead/lag of flow vs price.

**Graph.** G3 — large-holder net flow vs SUT price, daily (hourly for May 16–20).

### Supporting evidence
- **A near-daily, industrial-scale pipeline ran through the window.** SRC
  `0xaaA4D5dD…cc2c` (**top-10 holder #4**, 13.1M / 5.51%) sent **16,580,000 SUT** to DST
  `0x7CC2F891…F7ad` during May 1–17 — **2.8× the volume recorded at handoff**.
- **DST is a conduit, not a holder**: IN 19,007,099 / OUT 16,523,928 in the same window;
  current balance ~18,604 SUT. The tokens did not stay.
- **A third hop exists**: 15,200,000 SUT (92% of DST outflow, 29 tx) → HOP3
  `0xe6e7ec8d…ff85`.
- **Scale dwarfs all venues.** 16.58M SUT ≈ **$11.1M** at the pre-crash price, versus a
  ~$91.7K pool — two to three orders of magnitude larger than observable liquidity. Even a
  few percent reaching a market would be decisive.
- **Timing coincidence.** Outflows ran daily May 1–15, then **stopped on May 16 — the day
  before crash onset** — resuming only with a single 1,000,000 SUT move on **May 19**, the
  day after the low.
- **Cluster includes a second top-10 holder.** `0xf46e16da…7bc8` (#5, 10.3M / 4.31%) sends
  into HOP3.
- **Circular recycling**: 1,271,722 SUT returned DST→SRC over 42 transactions.

### Contradictory evidence — decisive against the concentrated-dump reading
- **MEASURED 2026-09-30 (Q1 resolved): the chain terminates in a retail fan-out, not a
  market sale.** In May 1–25 HOP3 received **15,200,471 SUT in 31 transfers** and sent
  **15,332,008 SUT out in 34,349 transfers** — average **~446 SUT** (≈$270) each. The largest
  single May recipient took **158,598 SUT (~1%)**. **No DEX pool, router, or identifiable
  exchange deposit address appears among the top recipients.**
- **HOP3's behavioural signature is distribution, not selling**, in both periods sampled.
  Jun 19–Sep 30: **8 inbound transactions → 1,994 outbound to 784 distinct recipients**,
  median **1,876 SUT** (≈$750). The May pattern is the same shape at ~17× the transaction
  volume.
- **No cluster address is a labelled exchange, pool, or LP.** All are unlabelled EOAs.
- **No DEX Swap by any cluster address has yet been demonstrated** (Q2 open). Transfers
  between wallets are **not sales**.
- **The pipeline stopped *before* the crash, not during it.** A seller dumping into a crash
  would show *rising* outflow through May 17–18. Observed outflow on May 16, 17 and 18 is
  **zero**. This is at least as consistent with "stopped because of the crash / upstream
  halt" as with "caused it".
- On-chain **transfers cannot distinguish** a sale, an internal rebalance, a custody move, or
  a user payout.

**Limitations.** Wallet **roles are UNKNOWN** — no public labels exist and none may be
invented. The 2.8× correction means earlier reasoning built on ~5.92M was working from
incomplete data. Crucially, **a fan-out to retail addresses does not prove the absence of
selling** — it relocates the question from one large seller to thousands of small ones, whose
behaviour is **not yet measured** (Q16). Whether any recipient sold into the pool is pending
(Q2).

### Status: **split by variant**

| Variant | Status | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| **H3a — concentrated holder dumped into the market** | **REJECTED** | The chain terminates in 34,349 retail transfers averaging ~446 SUT, with no venue address among top recipients. The tokens were dispersed, not sold by one holder. |
| **H3b — large-scale distribution created diffuse sell pressure** | **INCONCLUSIVE** | ~1M SUT/day (≈$600K/day) was delivered into retail hands against a ~$91.7K pool. If even a small fraction sold promptly it would exceed pool depth daily. **Mechanism is quantified; recipient behaviour is unmeasured.** |

> **Large flow is PROVEN. Concentrated selling is now REJECTED. Diffuse selling is
> UNMEASURED.** The investigation's centre of gravity moves from H3 to **H8/H11** — the
> question is no longer "did an insider dump?" but "did thousands of recipients sell what
> they were given?"

**Missing evidence.** **Q16 (new, highest value)** — sell-through rate of the May fan-out
recipients. **Q2** — any pool inflow attributable to cluster or recipient addresses. **Q12** —
movements of holders #1, #3, #6, #7. **Q15** — cause of the May 15 +44% spike on 6× volume.

## H4 — Exchange-access deterioration

**Statement.** Loss of exchange access (delistings, suspended deposits/withdrawals) reduced
liquidity and accessibility, causing or amplifying the decline.

**Mechanism.** Delisting → forced/panic exit before the deadline, and permanent reduction of
venue depth → higher price impact for identical flow.

**Why plausible.** SUT has demonstrably lost venues: GOPAX (2025), MEXC (no longer trading),
Gate TR (2026-04-01). Post-event, ~58% of volume sits on a single venue.

**Required data.** Timestamped listing/delisting/suspension notices **with contract-level
identity**; per-venue volume and depth around each event.

**Source.** Exchange announcements (Tier 2); per-venue market data.

**Calculation.** Event study: abnormal return and volume change in [−5d, +5d] around each
**identity-verified** venue event.

**Graph.** G4 — exchange events overlaid on price/volume; per-venue volume share over time.

### Supporting evidence
- **Gate TR** (`PAIR_VERIFIED`, SUPERTRUST SUT/TRY): announced 2026-03-19, services ceased
  **2026-04-01 03:00 UTC**. A genuine, identity-verified access loss ~6 weeks before the crash.
- **GOPAX** terminated support (2025) — removed the Korean-won on-ramp for a
  Korea-centred retail base.
- **MEXC** no longer trades SUT (API returns "invalid symbol"; no delisting notice found).
- Venue concentration after these losses raises fragility (feeds H2).

### Contradictory evidence
- **Gate TR's effective date (2026-04-01) is ~6 weeks before the crash.** DEX data shows
  price *recovering* into mid-May (0.5537 on Apr 30 → 0.6920 on May 15) — i.e. **no sustained
  decline followed the Gate TR event**. It cannot be the proximate trigger.
- **No identity-verified venue event is dated within 2026-05-16 → 2026-05-20.**

**Limitations — and a critical trap.**
> The handoff records a **BitMart** event with **"withdrawal closed 2026-05-16"** — exactly
> at crash onset, and exactly the day the wallet pipeline stopped. But
> `sut-coin-research` v2 established that **BitMart's SUT was Sanity United, not
> SuperTrust**. The notice could not be retrieved this cycle (**Cloudflare 403**), so the
> identity is **NOT VERIFIED**.
>
> If it *is* SuperTrust, H4 likely becomes the leading hypothesis. If it is Sanity United,
> then adopting it would manufacture a **completely spurious root cause at exactly the right
> date**, apparently corroborated by a coincidental on-chain correlation. **This single
> unresolved question can flip the case in either direction.**

Also unavailable: per-venue May volume (Q6); CEX depth (Q7).

### Status: **INCONCLUSIVE** — *blocked on an identity question (Q3), not on analysis*

**Missing evidence.** **Q3 (highest priority)** — BitMart contract identity. Q6 — per-venue
volume May 16–20. Q8 — any venue notice dated May 15–20. MEXC delisting date.

## H5 — Company / SuperSave / settlement flows

**Statement.** Company-controlled economic mechanisms (SuperSave settlements, buybacks,
credit locks, payouts) created net SUT supply into the market, or changed flow timing, in a
way that caused or amplified the decline.

**Mechanism.** If user balances are settled in SUT and users convert to cash, the company's
settlement schedule becomes a recurring supply stream. Changing its rate changes market
supply irrespective of demand.

**Why plausible.** The project published a **2026-03-30 "Short-Term SUT Value Surge
Strategy"** proposing a **60% lock of users' remaining credit balance**, **lower weekly
settlement**, and an expected **reduction in circulating quantity** — an explicit, dated,
company-controlled supply intervention six weeks before the crash. It also published buyback
offers tied to joining SuperSave.

**Required data.** Settlement records; SuperSave user balances and conversion volumes;
buyback execution records; company wallet map; treasury flows.

**Source.** Company systems — **no access**. Partial on-chain proxies only.

**Calculation.** Net company-attributable SUT supply per day vs price; pre/post analysis of
the 2026-03-30 intervention.

**Graph.** G6 — company interventions on the event timeline; net settlement flow vs price.

### Supporting evidence
- The 2026-03-30 strategy is a **documented, dated, company-controlled supply intervention**
  preceding the crash.
- **Circumstantial on-chain consistency:** the cluster's fan-out signature (8 large inflows →
  1,994 payouts to 784 recipients at ~$750 median) **is structurally what a platform payout
  system looks like**. DST also holds **14.7M MSQ**, the MSQUARE GLOBAL token.
- The near-daily May cadence (0.1M–2.0M SUT every day) resembles a **scheduled settlement
  process** more than discretionary trading.

### Contradictory evidence
- **No company wallet has been identified.** The MSQ holding and payout-shaped behaviour are
  **evidence, not proof** — anyone can hold MSQ, and fan-out patterns are not unique to
  companies.
- The company **publicly denies** the MSQUARE relationship (2026-08-08, notice 4615).
- Nothing links any observed address to SuperTrust, SuperSave, or SoloPay by public evidence.

**Limitations.** **No access to SoloPay, SuperSave, production systems, or settlement
records.** Every quantitative element of this hypothesis is unmeasurable from outside. The
on-chain resemblance to a payout system is **suggestive only** and must never be stated as
an identification of company wallets.

### Status: **DATA UNAVAILABLE**

**Missing evidence.** Q13 — any settlement/merchant/payment data. Q14 — whether the
2026-03-30 intervention changed on-chain behaviour. Published company wallet map. Buyback
execution evidence.

## H6 — Supply / distribution changes

**Statement.** A change in SUT supply (issuance, unlock, release) caused or amplified the
decline.

**Mechanism.** New sellable supply without matching demand → price falls.

**Why plausible.** Supply-counting changes have already driven a headline metric (the Feb
2026 rank jump).

**Required data.** `totalSupply` over time; Mint/Burn events; lock-up contract state; holder
balance distribution over May.

**Source.** Polygon RPC / Blockscout (`CONTRACT_VERIFIED`).

**Calculation.** Dated supply table from chain state and contract events across May 2026.

**Graph.** G6 — supply events on the timeline.

### Supporting evidence
- Distributed float can change without any token-supply change, via holder behaviour — the
  §7.2 pipeline is precisely such a distribution mechanism (16.58M SUT circulated through the
  cluster in May, ~8.8% of the 188.4M "circulating" figure).

### Contradictory evidence — decisive for token supply
- **Minting is not available** (F4) — verified, and re-confirmed by GoPlus.
- **`totalSupply` was and remains 238,403,732** (F10), re-checked 2026-09-30. **No supply was

- The contract is **not a proxy** (F7) — the supply logic cannot have been swapped.
- The Feb 2026 supply-counting change was a **data-provider accounting change**, not an

**Limitations.** "Supply" and "float" are different. The **50M lock-up question is

contract has been identified**, and holding tokens in an EOA is not a lock. The
188.4M / 46.6M project-reported conflict (C3) is unresolved.

### Status: **REJECTED** *(for on-chain token supply — no supply change occurred in May 2026)*
> The **float/distribution** question is **not** rejected — it is carried by H3. Do not
> conflate the two.

**Missing evidence.** Q4 — lock-up verification. Q9 — the 188.4M vs 46.6M conflict. Holder
distribution snapshots at May 1 / 16 / 18 / 25.

## H7 — Weak organic utility / adoption

**Statement.** SUT has little genuine economic demand, so its price was not supported by
utility and was vulnerable to any supply pressure.

**Mechanism.** Without recurring purchase demand from real usage, price rests entirely on
speculative flow. Any sustained sell pressure falls through an empty bid.

**Why plausible.** Multiple platforms show little or no measurable usage; the main benefit is
narrow; volume/market-cap ≈ 0.1%.

**Required data.** Merchants, payment counts and values, SUT-denominated payments, active
users, platform transactions, token velocity.

**Source.** Company systems (**no access**); on-chain proxies; app stores; CertiK activity
metrics.

**Calculation.** Usage vs market cap; token velocity = transfer value / market cap.

**Graph.** G7 — usage vs market cap. G8 — token velocity.

### Supporting evidence
- **Volume/market cap ≈ 0.1%**, persistently; similar-sized coins trade 10–200× more.
- **NEW — peer benchmark quantifies the adoption gap** (`PIP_Week_2_thesis.pdf` pp.3–7):
  against the #90–#110 band SUT is ~600× below median daily volume and ~110× below median
  volume/market-cap. On the qualitative factors that place peers in the band — published
  independent audit, identity-checked team, clear regulatory standing, **verifiable** usage,
  audited redeemable reserves for RWA claims, wide ownership, one consistent supply figure —
  **SUT satisfies none**, on available evidence. `SECONDARY` confidence.
- **NEW — RWA claim now has a concrete four-part test** (redeemability, custody, independent
  reserve audit, regulated issuer — the PAXG/XAUt standard). **SUT satisfies none of the four.**
- **Platform reality (measured):** ZERO PLUS app 50+ downloads; MOAD app listing not found;
  AI Studio 48 and SUT GOLD Connect — **no product found**; L2U and NATURUBOOK run by other
  companies. Only the SuperTrust app shows real traction (10K+ downloads).
- **Activity is thin relative to the holder base:** ~1,580 active users / 7d against
  54–56K holders ≈ **2.8% weekly activity** (CertiK, 2026-09-30).
- **Zero revenue for three years** reported (ESG경제, Oct 2025) — `SECONDARY`.
- The 30–50% fee discount applies **only to South Korean users of the operator's own
  platforms**.
- **Distribution ≠ demand.** HOP3's fan-out sends SUT *to* ~784 users; that is supply
  reaching holders, not evidence anyone *bought* SUT for utility.

### Contradictory evidence
- The SuperTrust app is genuinely live (10K+ downloads, 4.9★, 2.63K reviews).
- Company claims 2,000+ accepting stores and 40,000+ community members — **UNVERIFIED**.
- Real on-chain activity exists (5,799 tx / 7d; $1.61M transferred / 7d).

**Limitations.** **No measured payment data of any kind.** Company claims cannot be
verified or refuted. CertiK's "active users" methodology is unpublished. Critically, **H7 is a
structural/chronic condition, not a dated trigger** — weak utility was equally true on May 1
and May 16, so it cannot explain *why the crash happened on May 17* rather than any other day.

### Status: **INCONCLUSIVE**
> Best read as a **precondition/vulnerability** consistent with H2, not a trigger. A chronic
> condition cannot explain a dated event; it can explain why the event was severe.

**Missing evidence.** Q13 — merchants, payment count/value, active users, revenue. Time series
(all current figures are Sep 2026 snapshots — no May 2026 utility measurement exists).

## H8 — Weak token value capture

**Statement.** Even where ecosystem activity exists, it does not translate into SUT demand —
the token captures little of the economic value it is associated with.

**Mechanism.** If users can transact without net-buying SUT (paid *in* SUT, immediately
converting out; or discounts funded from float), activity produces **sell** pressure, not buy
pressure.

**Why plausible.** The observed cluster **distributes SUT to users**; if recipients convert
to cash, ecosystem activity is a structural supply source. This is the natural economic
reading of the fan-out — **but it is a hypothesis, not a measurement.**

**Required data.** Whether users **buy** SUT or **receive** it; conversion rates after
receipt; fee/revenue capture; whether discounts are funded by burns, buybacks, or float.

## H11 — Trust / information shock

**Statement.** A collapse in holder confidence — not tied to a single external event — caused
coordinated exit. A self-reinforcing loss of belief.

**Mechanism.** Accumulated unexplained crashes, delistings, legal reports and disclosure gaps
degrade confidence until some trigger causes many holders to exit simultaneously. Distinct
from H9: no single external catalyst is required.

**Why plausible.** The trust analysis catalogues **22 issues (5 CRITICAL, 9 HIGH)**. A
"victims' chat room" existed, showing organised holder distrust. Prior unexplained crashes
(−75% Sep 2025; to ~$0.44 Dec 2025) establish a pattern of confidence collapse.

**Required data.** Holder count changes; **number of distinct selling addresses** across the
event; community/social activity in the window; distribution of sell sizes (many small vs few
large).

**Source.** On-chain (holder deltas, unique addresses); social/community data (not gathered).

**Calculation.** **Discriminating test:** count *distinct* addresses selling on May 17–18.
- **Many small distinct sellers** → supports H11 (broad exit).
- **Few large sellers** → supports H3 (concentrated distribution).
This is a genuinely decisive test and is **feasible now** from pool Swap logs.

**Graph.** Unique seller count and sell-size distribution vs price, hourly, May 16–20.

### Supporting evidence
- A documented pattern of repeated unexplained crashes with **no project explanation** —
  precisely the condition that erodes confidence.
- Organised distrust existed (victims' chat room; the project responded with litigation).
- 22 catalogued trust issues.
- The May 17 pattern — a −62.7% intraday collapse with partial recovery, then a −72.5%
  continuation the next day — is **consistent with** a two-stage panic (initial shock, failed
  recovery, capitulation). **Consistent with is not evidence of.**

### Supporting evidence — the seller-breadth test has now been run (2026-09-30)
- **Sell transactions into the main pool rose 10–20× while the crash ran:** from a ~50–250/day
  baseline to **2,308 (May 17)** and **2,373 (May 18)**.
- **No single seller dominates.** Cluster addresses contributed **0 SUT (0.00%)**, and the
  largest pool senders are **router/aggregator contracts** bundling many users' trades — so
  the true number of economic sellers is **greater** than the 525 distinct addresses observed.
- **62% of distinct direct pool sellers were fan-out recipients** — i.e. the sellers were
  predominantly the dispersed retail base, exactly the population a confidence collapse would
  mobilise.
- The two-stage price path (−62.7% intraday May 17, partial recovery, then −72.5% May 18) is
  consistent with shock → failed recovery → capitulation.

### Contradictory evidence
- **No catalyst has been identified.** H11 posits confidence collapse; a collapse still
  usually has a trigger, and none is catalogued in the window (see H9).
- No community/social data for the window has been gathered — the *belief* limb of H11 is
  entirely unmeasured; only the *behaviour* limb has been observed.
- Broad selling is also consistent with a rational response to an unseen external event (H9)
  or a venue event (H4). **Breadth alone does not distinguish panic from informed exit.**

**Limitations.** H11 is the hardest hypothesis to falsify and the easiest to assert. What has
been measured is **broad-based selling**, not its *motive*. Attributing that breadth to "trust
collapse" rather than to an unobserved information event (H9) or venue event (H4) is **not
supported by the flow data alone**. **H11 and H9 remain entangled**; distinguishing them needs
the Track D window search.

### Status: **SUPPORTED** *(for the broad-exit behavioural limb only)*
> **Scope:** the evidence establishes that **many participants sold simultaneously into
> insufficient depth**. It does **not** establish *why*. The catalyst remains unknown, and
> H11's confidence-collapse interpretation is one of several readings of the same behaviour.

**Missing evidence.** The catalyst (Q8 / H9). Community/social and Korean chat-room activity
in the window. Holder-count time series across May. Router-level attribution to recover true
seller counts.

## H12 — Withdrawal of price support *(added 2026-09-30)*

**Origin.** `research-inputs/PIP_Week_2_thesis.pdf` p.7: *"With markets this thin, the price
can be pushed up cheaply, but it collapses when support stops. That is what the three past
crashes look like."* Reframed here neutrally and made testable.

**Statement.** Buy-side support (buybacks, market-support purchases, or other sustained
demand) held SUT's price above its unsupported level; the May 2026 collapse occurred when that
support was reduced or withdrawn, rather than because new selling appeared.

**Mechanism.** In a market with ~$91.7K of usable depth, continuous modest buying can hold a
price far above the level organic demand would sustain. If that buying stops, price falls to
the unsupported level — fast, and with no external news required. Under this model the
observed selling is a *consequence* of the support gap, not its cause.

**Why plausible.**
- **Documented, dated support programmes exist**: buyback at KRW 18,000 totalling KRW 10bn
  (2025-09-11); "1,000 SUT → 2,000 USDT" conditional on joining SuperSave (2026-01-16); the
  **2026-03-30 "Short-Term SUT Value Surge Strategy"**.
- **This is the only hypothesis that explains a crash with no catalyst** — and no catalyst has
  been found in the window (H9 DATA UNAVAILABLE).
- It is consistent with a measured session finding: the distribution pipeline **throttled ~70%
  on May 16**, the day before onset — a change in the operating regime immediately pre-crash.
- It would also explain the *repeating* pattern across three crashes with no identified cause.

**Required data.** Buy-side flow into the main pool (USDT in) across May; identity of buyers;
whether any consistent buyer ceased before May 17; buyback execution records; CEX buy-side
support (unavailable).

**Source.** Polygon RPC logs for the quote asset into pool `0x092295c9…e165`
(`CONTRACT_VERIFIED`); company buyback notices (`CLAIM` until verified on-chain).

**Calculation.** Daily buy-side volume and distinct buyer count, May 1–25; test for a
persistent buyer whose activity stops before onset; compare buy/sell imbalance pre- and
post-May 16.

**Graph.** G11 — pool buy-side vs sell-side flow, daily, with distinct-buyer count.

### Falsification criterion (stated before execution)
- **If buy-side flow was steady or rising into May 17**, H12 is **REJECTED** — no support was
  withdrawn.
- **If buy-side flow fell materially before May 17**, H12 becomes **SUPPORTED as a
  contributing mechanism** — and the identity/concentration of the departing buyer becomes the
  next question.
- **If buy-side was always negligible**, H12 is **REJECTED** — there was no support to withdraw,
  and the price level was never support-dependent.

### Supporting evidence
- Documented buyback/support programmes (above) — but these are **announcements**, and
  `data-source-contract.md` §3.7 requires on-chain verification before treating an announced
  action as having occurred. **None has been verified on-chain.**

### Contradictory evidence — the test was run and it failed the hypothesis

**Measured 2026-09-30** (quote-asset flow into/out of pool `0x092295c9…e165`, May 1–25):

| Date | USDT **into** pool (buy-side) | tx | USDT **out** (sell proceeds) | tx |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| May 14 | 82,372 | 264 | 76,411 | 225 |
| May 15 | 191,977 | 542 | 194,900 | 617 |
| May 16 | 35,113 | 139 | 32,501 | 162 |
| **May 17** | **1,364,855** | **2,801** | **1,379,239** | **2,829** |
| May 18 | 278,236 | 2,299 | 279,969 | 2,437 |
| May 20 | 209,646 | 1,717 | 192,054 | 1,718 |

1. **Buy-side did not withdraw — it peaked.** May 17 buy-side flow was the **month's absolute
   maximum** (1,364,855 USDT across 2,801 transactions). The pre-stated falsification
   criterion — *"if buy-side flow was steady or rising into May 17, H12 is REJECTED"* — is met.
2. **No persistent net-buyer is visible.** Buy-side and sell-proceeds are **near-identical
   every single day** (May 1: 89,007 vs 84,342; May 10: 196,530 vs 199,095; May 17:
   1,364,855 vs 1,379,239). This is balanced two-way flow, **not** a supporting buyer
   absorbing supply. There is no accumulation signature to withdraw.
3. **May 16 was simply a quiet day on both sides** (buys 35,113 / sells 32,501), not a
   buy-side-specific retreat.
4. **Buyers were present in force *during* the collapse** — 2,801 buy transactions on May 17 —
   and the price still fell 62.7% intraday. **Selling overwhelmed substantial active buying**,
   which strengthens **H2** and **H11** rather than H12.

**Limitations — important.**
- ⚠ **These figures conflate swap flow with LP add/remove flow.** Raw quote-asset transfers
  into a pool include Uniswap `Mint` (liquidity additions) as well as swap payments, and
  transfers out include `Burn`. Separating them is exactly **procedure C1 (Q5), still
  pending** — until then these are *gross pool flows*, not pure trading volume.
- **DEX limb only.** CEX-side support (buy walls on Gate, OTC buybacks) is **unmeasurable**,
  as is any buyback executed off this pool. H12 is rejected for the DEX limb; the CEX limb
  remains **DATA UNAVAILABLE**.
- Distinguishing "support buying" from ordinary buying requires identifying a persistent,
  concentrated buyer — none is apparent, but a concentrated buyer routing through aggregators
  would be masked by the router-attribution problem (§H3).
- **"Support withdrawal" and "manipulation" are different claims.** H12 tested only whether
  buying existed and stopped.

> **Neutrality requirement.** The source document characterises price/rank-pushing as
> *"market manipulation, which carries legal risk"*. **That is a legal characterisation, not an
> established finding**, and no competent authority has been shown to have made it about SUT.
> The word "manipulation" **must not** attach to H12's findings under any outcome.

### Status: **REJECTED** *(DEX limb — buy-side peaked rather than withdrew)*
**CEX limb: DATA UNAVAILABLE.**

> Added and tested within the same cycle. The hypothesis was worth adding — it was the only
> candidate that explained a crash with no catalyst — and the evidence rejected it on its own
> pre-stated criterion. **The catalyst gap therefore remains open, and is not explained by
> support withdrawal.**

**Missing evidence.** Separation of swap flow from LP flow (procedure C1 / Q5); CEX-side
support; on-chain verification of any announced buyback.

## Cross-hypothesis notes

### Complementarity, not competition
A coherent reading of the current evidence is that **H2 (thin depth) is the amplifier**, with
the trigger among **H3b / H4 / H9 / H11** — respectively unmeasured, identity-blocked,
unsearched, and unmeasured. **This is a framing, not a conclusion, and no combination may be
asserted as the root cause on present evidence.**

### How this cycle shifted the case
The chain-terminus measurement (Q1) **moved the centre of gravity away from H3a
(concentrated dumping — now REJECTED) toward H8/H11 (diffuse supply from mass
distribution)**. The question is no longer *"did an insider dump?"* but *"did thousands of
recipients sell what they were given, into a pool too thin to absorb it?"* That question is
**measurable on-chain and has not been asked yet**.

### What the evidence now establishes, and what it does not

**Established (measured, 2026-09-30):**
- The crash was **not** market-wide (H1 rejected).
- The crash was **not** a concentrated insider dump through the traced chain (H3a rejected;
  cluster contributed 0.00% of pool sells).
- The crash **was** a broad, multi-participant sell event — 10–20× the normal number of sell
  transactions (H11 broad-exit limb supported).
- The selling **overwhelmed available depth by more than an order of magnitude** — 2.46M SUT
  into a ~$91.7K pool over two days (H2 supported as amplifier).

**Not established — the central remaining gap:**
> **What made thousands of holders sell on 2026-05-17.** No catalyst has been identified. H4
> is identity-blocked, H9 is unsearched, and H11's *motive* limb is unmeasured. **The
> mechanism of the collapse is now well evidenced; its trigger is not.**

### The three questions that most move the case now
1. **Q3 — is the BitMart 2026-05-16 event SuperTrust or Sanity United?** Now the **single
   highest-value question**: it is the only catalogued candidate catalyst at the right date,
   and it is either the answer or a trap.
2. **Q8 — any information event in 2026-05-15→20?** The catalyst gap is now the case's centre.
3. **Q16 — true recipient sell-through**, including router- and CEX-mediated sales. Only the
   0.67% direct-transfer floor is known.

### What must never happen
- No AI-generated overall root-cause verdict.
- No wallet role assigned without public evidence (`UNKNOWN` is the default and current
  state for every address in this case).
- No promotion of timing correlation to causation — including the May 16 pipeline halt, which
  is the most seductive correlation in the dataset.
